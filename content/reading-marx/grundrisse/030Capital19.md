We have seen that it is in the nature of capital to maximise surplus labour and surplus productivity. This nature manifests itself through *competition*, through which the “inner tendency of capital appears as a compulsion exercised over it by *alien capital* [i.e. by *other capitals*], which drives it forward [...].

Marx attributes to Edward Gibbon Wakefield1 the proposition (in his annotated edition of *Smith’s Wealth of Nations*, published in numerous volumes in the 1830s) that economics has never properly theorised the notion of “free competition”; that, rather, they have only conceived of it in its *negative* sense: “i.e. as negation of monopolies, the guild system, legal regulations, etc.”
